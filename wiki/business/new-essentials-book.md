# The New Essentials of Sports Nutrition

His sports nutrition book. Due **late 2028**. Source: draft **v5** in Drive (Social Media Share › Book Copies,
Oct 12 2026). Publisher line in the draft: BD Solutions LLC. The concepts in short: [Coaching methods](coaching-methods.md).
Book breadcrumbs for social: [Coach Bronson content](coach-bronson-content.md) (that chapter map came from the May
draft, so its chapter numbers may not match v5).

How much was read: everything from the Introduction to Appendix A. Ch3 and Ch8 were read at the section level (main
points, not every paragraph). The references section was not reviewed.

## The book's argument in one paragraph

Metabolism is **fuel and function**, not a furnace. Train the athlete's body to store, reach, make and use its own
energy (true metabolic flexibility) and the athlete performs about the same at a **lower physiological cost**, with a
wider **longevity band**. Ketogenic nutrition is the main tool; carbohydrate is a targeted tool used at the smallest
dose that solves a named problem. Track macros in grams against lean mass, not calories.

## Chapter by chapter

**Introduction / Who this book is for.** Most athletic careers last under 7 years. His story: CrossFit gym from 2014,
ketogenic from May 2018. His priorities: recovery → longevity → performance.

**1. Understanding Metabolism.** Fuel vs function; three systems of function (biology, physiology, neurology);
functional macros (protein) vs fuel macros (fat, carbs); ATP pathways. His updated **oxidative priority** order:
alcohol 1, exogenous ketones 2, protein 3, carbs 4, fat 5, endogenous stores as needed. "External substrates ALWAYS
interrupt internal processing." Priority is processing order, not preference. True metabolic flexibility = 10
processes across storage, access, production and use. "Eat to store for later use."

**2. Athlete-Centered Nutrition.** Two pillars: physiological resilience and ketogenic nutrition. Adaptive economy.
The longevity band.

**3. The Basics of Nutrition.** Protein 2–3+ g/kg is safe; total daily intake matters more than timing; carbohydrate
is not essential; glycemic variability; nutrient density of meat; the "ADEQUATE" idea.

**4. What Is a Ketogenic Diet, Really?** History (Rollo 1797, Banting 1863, Salisbury 1888, Wilder 1921, Atkins
1972); the variations of keto and where he stands; carbs are used to solve specific problems.

**5. Common Ketogenic Diet Myths.** High fat, chasing ketones, "unsustainable", low T3, cortisol (normalizes in 4–6
weeks), "keto is horrible for athletes". His analysis of 45 studies: no performance decline after about 30 days.

**6. What Is Ketogenic "Fat" Adaptation?** Phases: fat-adapted (1–2 weeks), keto-adapted (2–6 weeks),
keto-optimized (6–12+ weeks). 6–12 weeks minimum.

**7. What Science Gets Wrong.** Ketosis is not adaptation. The average study lasts 34 days; only 11 run past 8 weeks.
Washouts. Movement economy (critique of Burke 2017; Dant et al. 2025; Prins 2019). Glycogen 25–40% lower. Insulin is
anti-catabolic. Studies rarely report electrolytes.

**8. How Keto Impacts Adaptive Economy.** Fat oxidation, glycogen sparing and AGEs, lactate, the crossover point, the
CNS, the anabolic environment, inflammation, fatigue resistance (central governor), overtraining syndrome. Summary:
similar output at lower cost, wider longevity band.

**9. Transitioning to a Ketogenic Diet.**
- Who struggles most: endurance athletes least, CrossFit/mixed-modal moderate, strength/power most (they must rewire
  fat use in daily life). "Sorry, Crossfitters."
- Expect a performance drop for the first several weeks: energy drops, reduced endurance and strength, keto flu,
  mental frustration.
- Electrolytes: sodium 3–5 g/day while adapting, 2–3 g/day after; potassium 3–4 g; magnesium 300–500 mg. Pre-load
  1–2 g sodium 1–2 h before long or hard sessions; 500–1,000 mg/h during long or hot ones. Replace losses, don't aim
  for excess.
- Taper carbs through Phase One so Phase Two starts at the target; don't keep adding carbs back for temporary fatigue.
- Exogenous ketones (BHB) during adaptation, daily and/or 20–30 min before training; the benefit fades over time.
- Fat at least 1 g per lb of lean mass, more if very lean. Treat the phase as base-building; plan recovery days;
  hydrate.
- Start in a de-load or off-season; within 6 weeks of competition or in-season, go much slower.
- Once keto-optimized: more endurance, better recovery, steadier energy, fed or fasted performance, fewer cravings,
  less GI distress.

**10. Macronutrient Recommendations.**
- Calories don't describe how nutrients are digested, stored or used. "You don't need calories. You need nutrition."
  Calorie counting is a SWAG: labels off 20%+, wearables unreliable, TEF ignored, stacked estimates.
- **Simple macro formula:** lean mass in lb → grams. Protein **at least** that many grams; carbs + fat combined **no
  more** than that. One number to remember. Example: 200 lb at 15% body fat → 170 g protein minimum, 170 g carbs+fat
  maximum.
- At least 100 g fat/day for athletes (even petite women); about 50 g carbs or less to stay fat-adapted.
- Move toward the targets over time; change one thing at a time.
- Worked example, 185 lb athlete: baseline 185P/135F/50C. Gaining fat → cut carbs to 25 g first. Losing too much →
  add fat (150 g), keep protein. Performance down → check hydration, electrolytes, sleep, load and adaptation before
  blaming carbs. Sample meal plans with no pre/intra/post snacks, on purpose.
- He hasn't used calorie counting with clients in over five years.

**"You Might Need More Fat"** (numbered 112 in the draft; sits between the two Sport Specific chapters).
- Not eating enough fat is a top reason athletes quit the transition. People with lots of body fat need less dietary
  fat; lean athletes need more ("butter chuggers" won't lose fat). Angus Barbieri's 382-day fast as the extreme case.
- Athletes are leaner, train harder and carry more lean mass, so they need more fat: **1.25–1.5 g fat per lb** of lean
  mass or body weight (vs about 1 g for non-athletes). Example: 150 lb athlete → 188–225 g fat.

**12. Sport Specific Recommendations.**
- The adaptive economy makes sport nutrition simpler: the same daily structure works across sports; daily nutrition is
  the main performance strategy.
- Low insulin raises sodium loss, so sodium may matter more than added carbs.
- **Two rules for carbs:** minimum effective dose, and carbs mainly as a recovery tool.
- **Prolonged endurance (about 90 min–2 h+):** start at 10–20 g carbs per hour (a study in triathletes: 10 g/h
  prevented hypoglycemia and raised time to exhaustion about 22%). Toward 3 h+, more may help; standard guidance
  (30–60 g/h, up to 90 g/h) stays an option to test. Some carnivore ultrarunners use none. Test under similar
  conditions; check GI tolerance.
- **Holding body mass for sport:** first add fat with adequate protein; if fullness or food fatigue blocks it, add
  modest, low-GI whole-food carbs in post-training meals; adjust by body-composition trend.
- **Closely scheduled events:** higher (relative) carbs soon after an event, tapering toward none before the next.
  Low performance doesn't prove low glycogen; check intake, load and sleep first.
- **Evaluating the response:** define the problem and the outcome, change one thing, keep using it only if it earns
  its place.

**13. Profiles of Keto-Adapted Athletes.** Why few elite athletes talk about keto (endorsements, team rules).
Profiles written: Alessandro Medeiros (ultra triathlon/ultrarunning, carnivore), Michael Andrew (Olympic swimmer),
Ryan Talbot (decathlete, carnivore), Tim Olson (ultrarunning), Zach Bitter (ultrarunning; about 40 g carbs/h in
races), Dave MacLeod (climbing), Robbie Moyles (CrossFit Masters, carnivore), Jocelyn Rylee (CrossFit coach), Robert
Sikes (natural bodybuilding), Alex McDonald (five fasted marathons in five days), Jonathan Griffiths (bodybuilding,
carnivore). Headings with no profile yet: Anthony Chaffee, Natalie Grasso.

**Appendix A: Overtraining blood tests and markers.** Ranges and what each says about overtraining: ferritin,
hemoglobin, hematocrit, vitamin D (40–60 ng/mL), omega-3 index (≥8%), hs-CRP, CK, cortisol, fasting glucose,
fasting insulin (athlete optimal 2–5), thyroid (low free T3 with normal TSH = overreaching or energy deficit),
cortisol:testosterone ratio (>30% rise from baseline is a red flag), glutamine, urea/uric acid/ammonia, sex hormones.
Field tests: exercise stress test, VO2 max ranges by age and sex, AFib, EKG, **HRV-CV** (SD ÷ mean × 100; <3% very
stable, 3–5% normal, 5–7% unstable, 8–10% persistent = concern) and a **repeated heart rate recovery test** (same
effort each time; 1-min drop and its slope over weeks). Both tied to the adaptive economy and longevity band.
Medical disclaimer.

## Editing issues in the v5 draft (Ch9 to Appendix A)

These are for his editor pass; none are decisions.

**Structure**
- Two "Sport Specific Recommendations" chapters: an older Ch11 and a rewritten Ch12. The "More Fat" chapter is numbered
  "112". The old Ch11 looks ready to delete and the rest to renumber.
- Ch9: "Consider Your Schedule", the modality table and the hit list each appear twice (once as a plain-text table).
  "Symptoms of Transition" is used as two headings; the second is really the fixes.
- Old and new versions of the same paragraph sit back to back: Ch9 "Gradually Reduce Carbohydrate Intake"; Ch10's
  opening, "Simple Macro Formula" and the ALERT about snacks; the Barbieri paragraph; Ch12's 10–12 g/h paragraph
  repeats in "Evaluating the Individual Response".
- Tracked-change residue: "yYears oOld", "BRobert", "RHe", "shouldmust", "athletespeople need to betarget",
  "A proper method would We measure".

**Numbers that disagree**
- Fat: Ch9 says at least 1 g/lb lean mass; Ch10's formula caps fat + carbs at 1 g/lb lean mass; the More Fat chapter
  says athletes need 1.25–1.5 g/lb. A 150 lb athlete at 188–225 g fat is over the Ch10 cap.
- Ch10 has two baselines: "1.25 g protein and 1 g fat/carbs per lb lean mass" (165 lb → 206P/150F/15C) and the simple
  formula (protein = lean mass g). Scenario 3 (150F + 50C = 200 g) is also over the 185 g cap.
- Robbie Moyles: heading says 62, text says 59. "Michael Andrews" heading vs "Michael Andrew" in the text.
- Appendix: hs-CRP "3.0 mg/L = High" should be ">3.0"; HRV-CV bands skip 7–8%; HRR "20 bpm drop" should be ">20";
  the slope example divides by 4 for weeks 1→4 (3 intervals, so −3.3/week, or say "4 tests"); copyright line appears
  as both 2025 and 2028.

**Typos and gaps**
- "fate intake" (fat), "Alex Mcdonald", "Here Below". Empty profiles for Anthony Chaffee and Natalie Grasso.

**Against his own writing rules** ([Writing and brand rules](../rules/writing-and-brand-rules.md))
- Ch10 says "the most transformative aspect of my practice". His rule: the business is never a "practice".
- Food as fuel: "Real food fuels real results", "under-fueling", "fueling" (Ch10, More Fat, Ch12). "Fuel macros" and
  "fuel and function" are his own terms and fine.
